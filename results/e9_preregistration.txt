E9 PRE-REGISTRATION: does the length bias hold for a 1B model on a different dataset?
Written 2026-11-24, before any E9 run. Nothing in this file may change after the first E9 result exists;
any later addition goes below a dated line marked "POST-HOC".

PURPOSE
E4, E7 and E8 used one small model (SmolLM2-135M) and one dataset (Alpaca). E9 tests whether the
predicted shortening under per-answer and small-group averaging holds for a model about eight
times larger, from a different family, on a different instruction dataset.

SETUP
- Model: TinyLlama/TinyLlama_v1.1 (1.1B parameters, Llama 2 architecture and tokenizer, Apache-2.0).
- Data: databricks/databricks-dolly-15k (CC BY-SA 3.0), shuffled with seed 0. Prompt in the Alpaca
  format, with the context field (if any) as the input; prompt masked; the response plus one
  end-of-text token carry the loss. Examples with more than 512 tokens in total are skipped.
  The first 12,000 remaining examples are used for training and the next 1,000 are held out.
- Fine-tuning with LoRA adapters (rank 16, alpha 32, no dropout) on all attention and MLP
  projection matrices; the base weights are frozen. Reason: full fine-tuning with AdamW does not
  fit in the 16 GB of one T4 GPU. The theory does not depend on how the parameters are updated.
- 32 answers per update, 1 epoch (375 updates), AdamW learning rate 2e-4, no weight decay, warm-up
  over the first 1/30 of updates, cosine decay to 10%, fp16 mixed precision, gradient
  checkpointing, no gradient clipping.
- Methods: per-answer (micro1), micro-4 (micro4), constant divisor (fixed). By Theorem 1, micro-4
  has the same expected gradient as the global average with 4 answers per update.
- Seeds: 300, 301, 302, 303 (4 seeds). Within a seed all methods share the data order and the
  sampling seed.
- 1,000 held-out prompts sampled per run (temperature 1, no top-k or top-p, at most 512 new
  tokens); every answer length is saved.

PRIMARY ENDPOINTS
For each seed s and method m in {micro1, micro4}:
  D_m(s) = 100 x (mean generated length of fixed - that of m) / that of m     [percent]
  (positive means method m gives shorter answers, as the theory predicts)

H1: mean D_micro1 > 0.   H2: mean D_micro4 > 0.
Each is a one-sided paired t-test over the seeds (t distribution with n-1 degrees of freedom).
The two p-values are Holm-corrected; each hypothesis passes if its Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.

Wording of outcomes:
- pass: "With a 1.1B model on Dolly, <method> shortens answers by X% (interval) relative to the
  constant divisor."
- fail: "No detectable shortening under <method> with a 1.1B model on Dolly (interval)."
  Reported as written.

INCOMPLETE RUNS
A crashed run, or one stopped by Kaggle time or quota limits, is rerun with the same seed (the
script resumes, including in a later week). If fewer than 3 complete seed pairs exist for a
comparison when the experiment is closed, that hypothesis is reported as INCOMPLETE, with the
interval from the pairs that exist. No seeds beyond 300-303 are added.

EXPECTED SIZE (stated before the run; descriptive, not tested)
For Dolly, E0's prompt-blind limits are -73% (per-answer) and -33% (groups of 4). In E4 the real
shifts were about 1/4 to 1/3 of their limits. Expected: per-answer about -15% to -30%, micro-4
about -5% to -15%, both relative to the constant divisor.

SECONDARY (descriptive, no pass/fail)
S1. Size: the shifts relative to the constant divisor, their 90% intervals, and their share of the
    prompt-blind limits computed from the training answers.
S2. Held-out token-mean loss, each method minus fixed, mean and 95% interval.
S3. Clipping regime at this scale: share of updates whose gradient norm exceeds 1, and the
    gradient-growth exponent alpha.
S4. Per-prompt paired length differences and medians.
S5. Comparison with E4 (SmolLM2-135M on Alpaca): shifts as a share of their prompt-blind limits.
