E8 PRE-REGISTRATION: does the standard method drift at small batches, where the theory says it should?
Written 2026-11-23, before any E8 run. Nothing in this file may change after the first E8 result exists;
any later addition goes below a dated line marked "POST-HOC".

PURPOSE
The standard method (global: divide by the batch's total response tokens) is a ratio estimator whose
bias shrinks roughly as 1/B, where B is the number of answers per update. At B = 32 (E4, E7) the bias
was too small to see. E8 tests the theory's claim at B = 4: the standard method should shorten
answers relative to the constant divisor (fixed), which is unbiased at every batch size.

PREDICTION FROM THE THEORY (stated before the run)
- Prompt-blind theory limit at B = 4: learned mean 43.9 against 57.6, i.e. -23.8%.
- Real shifts were about 1/4 to 1/3 of the limit in E4 (consistent with the prompt-conditional
  form, which scales the shift by E[Var(L | prompt)] / Var(L)).
- The expected gradient of global at B = 4 is the same as that of micro4 in E4 (each normalises over
  4 answers), and the settling point does not depend on step size or learning rate (theory T10,
  E5 P1). So the shift should be close to E4's micro4 shift, -6.3% (2 seeds, not significant there).
- Expected range: about -4% to -8% for global against fixed.

SETUP (identical to E7 unless stated)
- SmolLM2-135M, Alpaca, same 16,000 training and 1,000 held-out examples, prompt masked.
- B = 4 answers per update (E7: 32). 2 epochs, so 8,000 updates.
- Learning rate 3.5e-5 (square-root scaling from 1e-4 at B = 32, the usual rule for Adam), AdamW,
  same warm-up (1/30 of updates) and cosine schedule, fp16. No gradient clipping.
- Methods: global (weight 1/N per token) and fixed (weight 1/(B mu), mu = 57.61).
- Seeds: 200, 201, 202, 203, 204, 205, 206, 207 (8 seeds). Within a seed both methods see the same
  batch order and the same sampling seed, so each seed gives one paired difference.
- 1,000 held-out prompts sampled per run (temperature 1, no top-k or top-p, at most 512 new
  tokens), every answer length saved.

PRIMARY ENDPOINT
For each seed s:
  D_len(s) = 100 x (mean generated length of fixed - that of global) / that of global   [percent]
  (positive means global is shorter, as the theory predicts)

H1: mean D_len > 0. One-sided paired t-test on the 8 seed differences, alpha 0.05
    (t distribution with n-1 degrees of freedom). One test, so no multiplicity correction.

Outcomes and wording:
- H1 passes: "At 4 answers per update the standard method shortens answers by X% (interval) relative
  to the constant divisor; the constant divisor's unbiasedness matters at small batches."
- H1 fails: "No detectable drift at 4 answers per update (interval). In this setting the standard
  method's bias stays negligible even at small batches, and the constant divisor's advantage
  remains theoretical." The paper reports this as written.

INCOMPLETE RUNS
A crashed run is rerun with the same seed (the script resumes). Fewer than 6 complete seed pairs
when Kaggle time or quota runs out: H1 is reported as INCOMPLETE, with the interval from the pairs
that exist. No seeds beyond 200-207 are added.

SECONDARY (descriptive, no pass/fail)
S1. Size: mean shift of global against fixed, its 90% interval, its share of the theory limit
    (-23.8%), and comparison with E4's micro4 shift (-6.3%).
S2. Held-out token-mean loss, fixed minus global, mean and 95% interval. The theory gives two
    opposite effects (global's bias cost against fixed's higher variance), so no direction is
    predicted.
S3. Step-to-step spread of gradient size (coefficient of variation), fixed against global;
    expected higher for fixed, by more than the 4% seen in E7.
S4. Per-prompt paired length differences, pooled within seed, and median lengths.
S5. Batch-size check of unbiasedness: mean length of fixed at B = 4 (E8) against fixed at B = 32
    (E7), and the same for global (different seeds and learning rates, so an unpaired Welch
    comparison). The theory expects fixed to move less than global.
